The Vision

Work is a calling,
not just a job.

Most Christian private schools compete on one axis: college-prep credentials in a faith environment. That is a legitimate value proposition. It is also a crowded, expensive, and difficult-to-differentiate market.

TDR is building something different. A school where the diploma is not the finish line — it is the launchpad. Where Biblical teaching, rigorous academics, and a clear path to economic self-sufficiency are not separate things, but a single, unified mission.

We believe every child is capable of something extraordinary. Our role is to plant the seed, nurture the roots, and watch them grow into something fruitful in their own life — for the glory of God and the good of this community.

“Do you see someone skilled in their work? They will serve before kings.”

Proverbs 22:29

The Protestant concept of vocation as calling, the biblical dignity of skilled labor, and the stewardship of God-given talent give TDR a faith narrative that secular programs cannot replicate. This is a competitive advantage, not a footnote.

The Growth Roadmap

Where we’re
headed.

TDR is not building a larger school. It is building a better one — a model that other institutions in Central Florida and beyond will point to as the standard for what faith-based education can accomplish when it takes economic outcomes seriously.

The three-phase roadmap below outlines where donor investment goes over the next four years. Each phase builds on the last and is designed to be self-funding within 36 months.

Your gift today accelerates Phase 1 — the foundation that makes everything else possible.

Phase 1 — Year 1

Foundation

Curriculum upgrade, accreditation pursuit, employer advisory board formation, and launch of first career pathway tracks. Low capital, high leverage.

Phase 2 — Years 2–3

Revenue & Growth

Employer workforce partnerships, adult evening certificate programs, summer intensives, and first grant applications. Programs become self-sustaining.

Phase 3 — Years 3–4

Scale & Differentiate

Enrollment growth to 300–500 students, expanded trade tracks, facility improvement funded by grants, and a published Graduate Outcomes Report annually.
